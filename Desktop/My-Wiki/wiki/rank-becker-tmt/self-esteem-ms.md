# Self-Esteem as Death-Anxiety Buffer: The 1997 Empirical Test

> Harmon-Jones, Simon, Greenberg, Pyszczynski, Solomon & McGregor (1997). *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 72*(1), 24–36. 706 citations.

## The Gap This Paper Fills

By 1997 TMT had two well-supported hypotheses but a conspicuous hole between them:

The **anxiety-buffer hypothesis** was confirmed: strengthening self-esteem reduces anxiety in response to direct, graphic death threats (elevated skin conductance, self-reported fear). Self-esteem works as a shield against explicit death stimuli.

The **mortality salience (MS) hypothesis** was also confirmed: subtle reminders of death produce *worldview defense* — harsher judgments of moral transgressors, increased in-group favoritism, preference for worldview-affirming targets. But this worldview defense is a *delayed*, *distal* response, not an immediate anxiety response.

The untested question: does self-esteem also reduce this delayed worldview defense? The mechanisms might be entirely different. This paper answers yes — and explains why.

## Three Experiments

### Experiment 1 — Manipulated Self-Esteem (n = 49)

**Design**: 2 (personality feedback: positive vs. neutral) × 2 (MS: death vs. TV) factorial.

Self-esteem was boosted via bogus positive personality feedback derived from a prior mass survey. Mortality salience was induced via two open-ended questions about one's own death and what happens physically when one dies (the standard "Projective Life Attitudes Assessment" paradigm). The dependent variable was worldview defense: preference for a pro-U.S. essay author over an anti-U.S. essay author.

**Results**: MS increased worldview defense among neutral-feedback participants (*t*(45) = 2.89, *p* < .007) but had *no effect* on positive-feedback participants (*t* < 1.0). The predicted Personality Feedback × MS interaction emerged on the author composite (*F*(1,45) = 4.29, *p* < .05). Increased self-esteem dissolved the standard MS effect.

### Experiment 2 — Dispositional Self-Esteem (n = 50)

**Design**: 2 (Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale: high [>75th percentile, *M* = 38.4] vs. moderate [25th–50th percentile, *M* = 30.4]) × 2 (MS: death vs. TV).

Screening excluded extremely low self-esteem participants (mildly depressed individuals who show *especially high* worldview defense after MS, confounding the comparison).

**Results**: MS increased worldview defense only among moderate-self-esteem participants (*t*(46) = 2.87, *p* < .007 for author; *t*(46) = 2.97, *p* < .006 for essay). MS had no effect on high-self-esteem participants (both *t*s < 1.0).

**Unexpected baseline finding**: In the *control* condition, high-self-esteem participants showed *more* worldview defense than moderate-self-esteem participants — they were already more invested in the cultural worldview before any death reminder. This is consistent with the idea that high-self-esteem individuals derive more self-worth from their culture and thus defend it more vigorously under normal circumstances. But after MS, they simply don't *increase* their defense, because the buffer is already operating at full capacity.

Both experiments converge: whether self-esteem is situationally elevated or dispositionally high, it short-circuits the worldview-defense response to mortality salience.

### Experiment 3 — The Mechanism (n = 48)

The dual-process model (developed concurrently, formalized by Pyszczynski et al. 1999) proposes that MS effects on worldview defense operate through death-construct accessibility: after MS, death-related thoughts are actively suppressed from focal consciousness, but they remain cognitively accessible just below the threshold; as suppression relaxes (after delay and distraction), accessibility increases, driving worldview defense.

**Hypothesis**: High self-esteem facilitates sustained suppression of death constructs following MS, preventing the delayed accessibility spike that triggers worldview defense.

**Design**: 2 (personality feedback: positive vs. neutral) × 2 (MS: death vs. TV), with word-fragment completion tasks administered *immediately* after MS and again after a *delay + distraction* (reading a Camus passage, additional questionnaires).

Word fragments were ambiguous: "D B \_\_" could be completed as DEAD or DEEP; "COFF\_ \_" as COFFIN or COFFEE. Death-related completions indexed accessibility of death constructs.

**Results** (key interaction, *F*(1,44) = 7.55, *p* < .009):

| Condition | Immediate | After delay |
|---|---|---|
| MS + neutral feedback | 1.00 | **2.64** |
| MS + positive feedback | 0.92 | 0.77 |
| Control + neutral | 0.75 | 0.75 |
| Control + positive | 0.92 | 0.75 |

The critical finding: in the neutral-feedback MS condition, death-construct accessibility *increased* dramatically after the delay (*t*(44) = 5.31, *p* < .001) — precisely the pattern the dual-process model predicts. In the positive-feedback MS condition, no such increase occurred (*t* < 1.0). High self-esteem maintained suppression across the delay interval, eliminating the usual accessibility surge.

MS participants reported more negative affect and more fear than controls regardless of self-esteem condition — showing the manipulation worked — but affect did not mediate the accessibility difference.

## The Two-Level Buffer Model

This paper establishes that self-esteem buffers death anxiety at *two distinct levels*:

**Level 1 — Acute anxiety response** (previously known): Self-esteem reduces self-reported fear and psychophysiological arousal when death imagery is immediate and salient.

**Level 2 — Distal worldview defense** (this paper): Self-esteem keeps death constructs suppressed in the post-MS interval, eliminating the delayed accessibility increase that would otherwise trigger worldview defense.

The mechanism at Level 2 is cognitive: high self-esteem facilitates sustained suppression of death-related constructs following a mortality reminder. Because death thoughts never surge in accessibility, the need for worldview-defense compensation never arises.

## Trait vs. State Self-Esteem

Both dispositional and experimentally boosted self-esteem reduced MS effects, but the papers' authors flag a key asymmetry. Dispositionally high self-esteem reflects long-standing, stable self-evaluations rooted in cultural embeddedness — these individuals defend their worldview more at baseline. Experimentally increased state self-esteem lacks this cultural depth; it may work primarily through a more immediate cognitive-suppression pathway rather than through genuine cultural investment.

This distinction matters for the broader TMT argument. Becker emphasized that self-esteem is not a free-floating good feeling but a *culturally contingent* sense of value — one earns it by living up to worldview standards. High dispositional self-esteem in that sense is a measure of how successfully one has internalized and performed one's cultural role. The experiments here test the buffer function of both types without fully resolving which mechanism does the work.

## Significance Within TMT

This study provides the first experimental evidence that the anxiety-buffer function of self-esteem extends from acute anxiety reduction to the suppression of distal, worldview-defense responses. It directly supports the central TMT proposition — that self-esteem and worldview function as an integrated cultural anxiety buffer — and adds mechanistic precision: the suppression of death-construct accessibility is the linking variable.

The finding also clarifies why moderately self-esteem individuals are the most sensitive to MS paradigms: they are invested enough in the worldview to show worldview defense when threatened, but insufficiently buffered to prevent the threat from penetrating. They are the experimental "sweet spot" — neither too defended nor too fragile.

## 한국어 번역

# 죽음 불안 완충제로서의 자존감: 1997년 경험적 검증

> 하몬-존스, 사이먼, 그린버그, 피슈친스키, 솔로몬, 맥그레거 (1997). *성격 및 사회심리학 저널, 72*(1), 24–36. 인용 706회.

## 이 논문이 메우는 공백

1997년까지 TMT는 두 가지 잘 지지된 가설 사이에 눈에 띄는 공백을 안고 있었다.

**불안 완충 가설**은 이미 확인되었다: 자존감을 강화하면 직접적이고 생생한 죽음 위협(피부 전도도 상승, 자기 보고 공포)에 대한 불안이 감소한다.

**사망 현저성(MS) 가설**도 확인되었다: 죽음에 대한 은근한 상기가 *세계관 방어*를 촉발한다—도덕적 위반자에 대한 가혹한 판단, 내집단 편애 증가, 세계관을 지지하는 대상에 대한 선호. 그러나 이 세계관 방어는 *지연된* 반응이다.

검증되지 않은 질문: 자존감이 이 지연된 세계관 방어도 감소시키는가? 이 논문은 그렇다고 답하며, 그 이유를 설명한다.

## 세 가지 실험

### 실험 1 — 조작된 자존감 (n = 49)

**설계**: 2(인격 피드백: 긍정 vs. 중립) × 2(MS: 죽음 vs. TV) 요인 설계.

가짜 긍정 인격 피드백으로 자존감을 높였다. 사망 현저성은 표준 '투사적 삶의 태도 평가' 패러다임으로 유도했다. 종속변수: 친미 에세이 저자에 대한 선호 vs. 반미 에세이 저자에 대한 선호(세계관 방어).

**결과**: MS는 중립 피드백 참가자의 세계관 방어를 증가시켰지만(*t*(45) = 2.89, *p* < .007), 긍정 피드백 참가자에게는 *아무런 효과가 없었다*(*t* < 1.0). 높아진 자존감이 표준 MS 효과를 해소했다.

### 실험 2 — 기질적 자존감 (n = 50)

**설계**: 2(로젠버그 자존감 척도: 높음 [>75백분위, *M* = 38.4] vs. 보통 [25–50백분위, *M* = 30.4]) × 2(MS: 죽음 vs. TV).

**결과**: MS는 보통 자존감 참가자들의 세계관 방어만을 증가시켰고(*t*(46) = 2.87, *p* < .007), 높은 자존감 참가자에게는 효과가 없었다(둘 다 *t* < 1.0).

**예상치 못한 기저선 발견**: *통제* 조건에서 높은 자존감 참가자들이 보통 자존감 참가자들보다 *더 많은* 세계관 방어를 보였다—죽음 상기 없이도 이미 자신의 문화에 더 깊이 투자되어 있었다. 그러나 MS 이후에는 방어를 *증가*시키지 않았다. 완충이 이미 완전 가동 중이었기 때문이다.

### 실험 3 — 기제 검증 (n = 48)

**가설**: 높은 자존감이 MS 이후 죽음 구성물의 지속적인 억제를 촉진하여, 세계관 방어를 촉발하는 지연된 접근성 급증을 막는다.

**설계**: 2(인격 피드백) × 2(MS), MS 직후와 *지연 + 주의 전환* 후에 단어 파편 완성 과제 실시.

단어 파편은 죽음 관련 단어("DEAD", "COFFIN")나 중립 단어("DEEP", "COFFEE")로 완성될 수 있도록 설계되었다.

**결과** (핵심 상호작용, *F*(1,44) = 7.55, *p* < .009):

| 조건 | 즉시 | 지연 후 |
|---|---|---|
| MS + 중립 피드백 | 1.00 | **2.64** |
| MS + 긍정 피드백 | 0.92 | 0.77 |
| 통제 + 중립 | 0.75 | 0.75 |
| 통제 + 긍정 | 0.92 | 0.75 |

핵심 발견: 중립 피드백 MS 조건에서 지연 후 죽음 구성물 접근성이 *급격히 증가*했다(*t*(44) = 5.31, *p* < .001)—이중 처리 모델의 예측과 정확히 일치한다. 긍정 피드백 MS 조건에서는 그런 증가가 없었다(*t* < 1.0). 높은 자존감이 지연 구간에 걸쳐 억제를 유지하여 접근성 급증을 제거했다.

## 2단계 완충 모델

이 논문은 자존감이 두 가지 별개의 수준에서 죽음 불안을 완충함을 확립한다:

**1단계 — 급성 불안 반응** (이미 알려짐): 자존감이 죽음 이미지가 즉각적이고 현저할 때 자기 보고 공포와 생리적 각성을 감소시킨다.

**2단계 — 원위 세계관 방어** (이 논문): 자존감이 MS 후 간격에 죽음 구성물을 억제 상태로 유지하여, 세계관 방어 보상을 촉발하는 지연된 접근성 증가를 제거한다.

## 기질 자존감 vs. 상태 자존감

기질적으로 높은 자존감은 문화에의 깊은 내재화를 반영한다—이 개인들은 기저선에서 세계관을 더 많이 방어한다. 실험적으로 높인 상태 자존감은 이런 문화적 깊이가 없다; 주로 인지적 억제 경로를 통해 작동할 수 있다. 베커가 강조했듯, 자존감은 자유로이 떠다니는 좋은 감정이 아니라 *문화적으로 조건 지어진* 가치감이다.

## TMT 내에서의 의의

이 연구는 자존감의 불안 완충 기능이 급성 불안 감소에서 원위 세계관 방어 반응의 억제까지 확장됨을 보이는 최초의 실험적 증거를 제공한다. 자존감과 세계관이 통합된 문화적 불안 완충 체계로 기능한다는 TMT의 핵심 명제를 직접 지지하며, 기제적 정밀도를 추가한다: 죽음 구성물 접근성의 억제가 연결 변수다.

또한 보통 자존감 개인들이 MS 패러다임에 가장 민감한 이유를 설명한다: 위협받을 때 세계관 방어를 보일 만큼 투자되어 있지만, 위협의 침투를 막기에는 불충분하게 완충되어 있다.
